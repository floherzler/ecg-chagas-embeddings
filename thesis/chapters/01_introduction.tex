\section{Clinical Motivation}
Chagas disease (ChD) is a neglected tropical disease caused by parasite \textit{Trypanosoma cruzi} infection, predominantly in Latin America.
It can progress to chronic Chagas cardiomyopathy, which is associated with conduction abnormalities, arrhythmias, and heart failure.

The infection with chagas disease starts with an acute phase with fever and mild symptoms.
After this phase, infected individuals enter the indeterminate phase, which can last for decades without symptoms while the ECG appears normal.
The chronic phase can develop in approximately 30\% of infected individuals, potentially leading to serious cardiac complications.

Early identification of infected individuals enables confirmatory serological testing and timely follow-up, but population-wide screening is challenging in resource-constrained settings.

\subsection*{Electrocardiogram as Screening Modality}
The 12-lead electrocardiogram (ECG) is inexpensive, widely available, and directly reflects cardiac electrical activity.
Since chronic Chagas disease can manifest with characteristic electrophysiological changes, the ECG is a plausible first-line screening modality to \emph{prioritize} who should receive confirmatory serology \parencite{jidling_screening_2023}.
Importantly, an ECG-based model is expected to be insensitive to early infection without cardiac involvement; in that setting the ECG may appear normal and serology remains the definitive test.

\subsection*{George B.~Moody PhysioNet Challenge 2025}
The experimental classification task in this thesis originates from the George B.~Moody PhysioNet Challenge 2025, which focused on detecting Chagas disease from the ECG \parencite{2025ChallengeCinC}.

The task is therefore interpreted as learning a \emph{risk score for Chagas-related ECG manifestations} (as represented by the available labels), rather than a universal screen for all infected individuals irrespective of disease stage.
The challenge emphasizes performance at a low false-positive rate, motivating metric-aligned modeling choices.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Operating point:} fixed-capacity screening (top $K\%$ flagged for follow-up; $K=5\%$ in the challenge setting).
  \item \textbf{Evaluation emphasis:} ranking quality under strict false-positive constraints.
  \item \textbf{Data sources:} large-scale CODE-15\%\parencite{ribeiro_code-15_2021} cohort (self-reported labels from Brazil), SaMi-Trop\parencite{ribeiro_sami-trop_2021} (clinically confirmed positives from Brazil), PTB-XL\parencite{wagner_ptb_xl} (confirmed negatives from Germany).
\end{itemize}



\section{Deep Learning for ECG Classification}
ECG-based detection requires modeling subtle, distributed waveform patterns across leads and time.
Deep learning methods, particularly convolutional architectures, can learn such representations directly from raw waveforms and have shown strong performance for ECG interpretation tasks \parencite{jidling_screening_2023}.

This thesis investigates the extent to which these models learn disease-relevant morphology versus dataset shortcuts, while developing strategies to ensure robustness under the practical constraints of strong class imbalance and domain shift.
Models are explicitly only trained on signal data wihtout introducing age, sex and other related metadata as features to avoid potential biases and aim for generalizability.

\section{Key Challenges}
Two properties of the training data complicate optimization and interpretation.

First, the severe class imbalance with only approximately 2~\% positives can cause standard learning objectives to be dominated by easy negatives.

\section{Contributions and Thesis Structure}
This thesis investigates practical strategies for improving model robustness under the constraints of severe class imbalance and dataset bias.
The core hypothesis is that careful data harmonization and metric-aligned objectives significantly influence the geometry of the learned embedding space, thereby determining downstream separability and generalization.
To test this, the experimental work is organized into three tracks utilizing embedding-space analysis as a primary diagnostic tool.

(i) The first track focuses on supervised classification, employing metric-aligned loss functions and controlled augmentations to optimize performance at low false-positive rates.

(ii) The second track explores supervised contrastive and prototype-based training methods to explicitly shape the embedding space, allowing for the quantification of representation quality.

(iii) The third track connects these representations to classification performance through downstream linear probing of pretrained encoders.
Across these tracks, three preprocessing regimes regarding bandpass filtering and scaling normalization appear as key experimental variables to balance data harmonization against the preservation of physiological variation.

The resulting models are evaluated on two levels: first, by their clinical ranking utility using metrics such as TPR@5\% and partial AUC; and second, via embedding diagnostics to detect mode collapse and dataset-specific clustering artifacts.
