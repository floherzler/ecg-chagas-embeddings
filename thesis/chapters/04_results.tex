\chapter{Results}
\label{ch:results}

This chapter summarizes empirical findings from the evaluation pipeline described in Chapter~\ref{ch:methods}.
All reported metrics are computed on the held-out test fold~4.
Results are organized along the same four-stage workflow:

(1) clinical task utility,

(2) embedding-space health,

(3) cross-model consistency and consensus-based sample selection, and

(4) plausibility assessment with ST-DFT-LRP.
\\
Accordingly, models carried into later stages are prioritized based on Stage~1/2 evidence, candidate ECGs are selected from Stage~3 consensus analyses, and channel-focused attribution summaries use the predefined representative lead setting for the XAI step.
Unless stated otherwise, results are reported per run and grouped by preprocessing regime and learning strategy.

\section{Stage 1: Clinical task utility}
Following the challenge protocol from which this task originates, TPR@5\% is used as the primary endpoint.
As described in Chapter~\ref{ch:background}, this is complemented by pAUC@5\% FPR, Average Precision (AP), TPR@10\%, and AUROC for a broader performance characterization.
% Per-run results are reported with compact model labels (\texttt{track / preprocessing / objective}) to keep the full model list readable.

\paragraph{Effect of preprocessing and learning strategy.}
Across all runs, performance differences are dominated by preprocessing regime.
The strongest model in this sweep is \texttt{t1-bp-focal} with TPR@5\%=0.4335, pAUC@5\%=0.3310, AP=0.2080, and AUROC=0.8388.
The strongest Track~3 representation-learning model is \texttt{t3-bp-sc-supstd}, which reaches the same TPR@5\% (0.4335) as \texttt{t1-bp-focal}, while slightly underperforming on pAUC@5\% (0.3256 vs 0.3310), AP (0.1962 vs 0.2080), and AUROC (0.8271 vs 0.8388).
For Track~1, the effect of adding softclipping (\texttt{bp-sc}) depends on objective: compared with matched \texttt{bp} runs, \texttt{rat} improves in all reported metrics, whereas \texttt{bce} and \texttt{focal} are lower in all reported metrics.
For Track~3, \texttt{bp-sc} improves over \texttt{bp} in all three objective-matched comparisons (\texttt{supstd}, \texttt{supmin}, \texttt{proto}), including the primary endpoint TPR@5\% (0.3844$\rightarrow$0.4335, 0.2980$\rightarrow$0.3587, 0.3372$\rightarrow$0.4022, respectively), with corresponding gains in pAUC@5\%, AP, TPR@10\%, and AUROC.
Within this single fold-4 sweep, \texttt{supstd} is the strongest Track~3 objective under each preprocessing regime (best TPR@5\% per regime: \texttt{t3-exp01-bp-supstd}=0.3844, \texttt{t3-exp03-bp-sc-supstd}=0.4335, \texttt{t3-exp04-bp-sc-norm-supstd}=0.1134), while \texttt{supmin}/\texttt{proto} remain below these values on the same endpoint.
In contrast, \texttt{bp-sc-norm} substantially degrades performance in both tracks (best values only 0.2213 TPR@5\% in Track~1 and 0.1134 in Track~3), consistent with over-harmonization removing clinically relevant ranking signal.
Full per-run values are reported in Table~\ref{tab:cls_per_run}.

\paragraph{Metadata random-forest baseline vs.\ deep learning.}
To contextualize the gain from waveform-level representation learning, a metadata-style random forest baseline (per-channel mean/std features only; Methods, Chapter~\ref{ch:methods}) was evaluated on the same fold~4 split.
Without using temporal morphology, this baseline reaches AUROC values of 0.7504 (\texttt{bp}), 0.7506 (\texttt{bp-sc}), and 0.7235 (\texttt{bp-sc-norm}), with corresponding PR-AUC values of 0.1318, 0.1322, and 0.1117.
In contrast, the best deep-learning runs in Stage~1 reach substantially higher AUROC and AP (e.g., \texttt{t1-bp-focal}: AUROC=0.8388, AP=0.2080), supporting the use of deep encoders beyond simple summary-feature baselines.
The RF summary metrics are listed in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:appendix_rf_metrics}, and feature-importance profiles are shown in Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:appendix_rf_importance}.

\paragraph{Rotation augmentation.}
Pairwise comparisons of otherwise matched runs (same track, preprocessing, and objective; with vs.\ without \texttt{+rot10}) show predominantly negative effects.
For the primary metric TPR@5\%, \texttt{+rot10} is lower in 5/6 pairs and higher in 1/6; the single improvement occurs for \texttt{t3-bp-supmin} (0.2980 $\rightarrow$ 0.3409).
Across supporting metrics, \texttt{+rot10} is lower in 5/6 pairs for pAUC@5\%, AP, and TPR@10\%, and lower in 6/6 pairs for AUROC.
The mean deltas across all six matched pairs are negative for every metric (TPR@5\%: $-0.0905$, pAUC@5\%: $-0.0608$, AP: $-0.0387$, TPR@10\%: $-0.1243$, AUROC: $-0.0655$).
Overall, rotation augmentation does not provide robust gains and is therefore not pursued further as a regularizer in this study.
Taken together, Stage~1 supports prioritizing non-rotated \texttt{bp} and \texttt{bp-sc} variants for the subsequent analyses.

\paragraph{Verified vs.\ self-reported labels.}
Using the predefined label-strength split (Methods, Chapter~\ref{ch:methods}), Table~\ref{tab:cls_split_by_group} compares verified cohorts (PTB-XL + SaMi-Trop) against CODE-15\%.
Because the reported overall scores are computed on the entire test fold~4, they are numerically dominated by CODE-15 (roughly 68{,}592 CODE-15 vs.\ 4{,}644 verified samples in this fold).
In Track~1, verified performance is consistently higher than CODE-15\% for \texttt{bp} and \texttt{bp-sc} at group level (median TPR@5\%: 0.506 vs 0.405 and 0.478 vs 0.407, respectively), while \texttt{bp-sc-norm} degrades both and reverses this ordering.
At per-run level (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:cls_tpr_split_per_run}), this pattern is visible in representative models such as \texttt{t1-bp-focal} (0.484 verified vs 0.436 CODE-15) and \texttt{t1-bp-sc-bce} (0.529 vs 0.407).
Two extreme cases stand out within Track~1 \texttt{bp}: \texttt{t1-bp-bce-rot10} and \texttt{t1-bp-rat-rot10} both reach very high verified TPR@5\% (0.7436), but simultaneously show \texttt{AUROC\_verified}=0.50 and \texttt{AP\_verified}=0.067.
This combination suggests a split-specific threshold effect without robust ranking quality and is flagged for later discussion rather than interpreted as genuine superiority.
In Track~3, \texttt{bp} shows the opposite tendency (median verified below CODE-15\%: 0.272 vs 0.325), but \texttt{bp-sc} restores a verified advantage (0.436 vs 0.401), and \texttt{bp-sc-norm} remains uniformly low.
Overall, the split indicates that preprocessing not only affects absolute utility but also strongly modulates transfer between verified and mixed-label subsets.

\paragraph{ECG quality confounding in CODE-15\%.}
Following the quality-analysis protocol defined in Chapter~\ref{ch:methods}, ECG quality was quantified with Zhao2018 categories.
Within CODE-15\%, quality categories are significantly associated with the Chagas label (Table~\ref{tab:code15_quality_chagas}).
Using the counts in Table~\ref{tab:code15_quality_chagas} and normalizing within each label column, positives contain fewer \emph{Excellent} recordings (57.9\% vs 72.0\%) and more \emph{Barely acceptable} (33.7\% vs 24.3\%) and \emph{Unacceptable} signals (8.5\% vs 3.8\%) than negatives.
This shift is statistically supported by both tests reported in the table (2$\times$2 Fisher, Excellent vs.\ not: OR=1.870, $p=3.94\times10^{-129}$; 3$\times$2 FFH Monte-Carlo: $p\approx 0.0001$).
This demonstrates a strong label--quality dependency that can act as a shortcut signal in both model scoring and qualitative embedding overlays.
Therefore, quality is treated as an explicit covariate in later visualizations and groupwise analyses.
For additional context, CODE-15 metadata correlation maps and pairwise plots are provided in Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:appendix_code15_corr}.

\section{Stage 2: Embedding-space diagnostics}
Embedding-space diagnostics are computed on the fixed probe subset (all positives plus twice as many negatives) to provide a comparable representation snapshot across runs.
The primary analyzed space is the encoder embedding (\texttt{enc}) for all tracks.
Metrics include GPU and CAC (collapse diagnostics and global class separation), and SAA where available (two-view consistency on the probe set).
Per-run embedding diagnostics are reported with compact model labels.

\paragraph{Effect of preprocessing and learning strategy.}
At run level, Stage~2 follows the same decision order as Stage~1: first compare model quality within the stable preprocessing regimes (\texttt{bp}, \texttt{bp-sc}), then use collapse diagnostics to reject unstable regimes (here \texttt{bp-sc-norm}).
Consistent with the screening objective, class~1 diagnostics are treated as primary for model selection in this stage, while class~0 diagnostics are used mainly as supporting context.

For \textbf{class-separation quality} (CAC$_1$), Track~1 remains competitive under both \texttt{bp} and \texttt{bp-sc} (best values: \texttt{t1-bp-bce-rot10}=65.56 and \texttt{t1-bp-sc-bce}=64.18). In Track~3, the strongest CAC$_1$ is reached by \texttt{t3-bp-sc-proto} (70.23), indicating that the \texttt{bp-sc} setting still supports strong downstream separability.

For \textbf{pair-consistency quality} (SAA), Track~3 \texttt{bp-sc-supmin} yields the strongest values (SAA$_0$=99.56, SAA$_1$=99.82), showing highly consistent local neighborhoods in the learned embedding space.

The \textbf{collapse check} is most evident in \texttt{bp-sc-norm}: GPU values approach 1.0 (e.g., \texttt{t3-bp-sc-norm-supstd}: GPU$_0$=0.999, GPU$_1$=0.999) while CAC$_1$ drops to roughly 35--38.
This reproduces the Stage~1 finding that \texttt{bp-sc-norm} is not a viable regime for robust screening models.

For GPU class asymmetry, the per-run table shows no strict track-level dominance of GPU$_0$ versus GPU$_1$.
In Track~1, GPU$_0<$GPU$_1$ is typical under \texttt{bp} and \texttt{bp-sc}, with the main reversals concentrated in \texttt{rat} runs (and minor reversals in \texttt{bp-sc-norm}).
Track~3 shows a similarly mixed pattern, including reversals in selected \texttt{supstd}/\texttt{supmin} variants and near-ties under \texttt{bp-sc-norm}.
This supports using GPU as a collapse indicator rather than a standalone class-priority metric.
Overall, the per-run embedding table supports prioritizing \texttt{bp} and \texttt{bp-sc} variants for subsequent consistency and plausibility analyses.
Full per-run values are reported in Table~\ref{tab:emb_per_run}.

\paragraph{Embedding health versus clinical utility.}
Figure~\ref{fig:overview_cac_tpr} relates embedding class-separation quality (CAC$_1$) to screening utility (TPR@5\%).
Across runs, stronger CAC$_1$ generally co-occurs with better downstream ranking performance, supporting CAC as a primary embedding-health indicator for model prioritization.
Table~\ref{tab:emb_tpr_corr} shows that among diagnostics available for all runs (\(n=24\)), CAC$_1$ has the strongest association with TPR@5\% (\(r=0.777\)), stronger than GPU$_0$/GPU$_1$ and slightly stronger than CAC$_0$.
SAA$_0$/SAA$_1$ are higher but are computed only for Track~3 (\(n=12\)), so they are reported as track-specific rather than all-run comparators.
In the top-right region of Figure~\ref{fig:overview_cac_tpr}, the leading candidates are \texttt{exp11} in \texttt{t3-bp-sc} (\texttt{t3-exp11-bp-sc-proto}: CAC$_1$=70.23, TPR@5\%=0.402) and \texttt{exp1}/\texttt{exp3}/\texttt{exp4} in \texttt{t1-bp} (\texttt{t1-exp01-bp-bce}, \texttt{t1-exp03-bp-focal}, \texttt{t1-exp04-bp-focal-rot10}) with TPR@5\%\(\approx\)0.429--0.433 and CAC$_1$\(\approx\)63.99--65.36.
An informative outlier is \texttt{t1-exp02-bp-bce-rot10}: high CAC$_1$ (65.56) but low TPR@5\% (0.147), indicating that strong global class-separation alone is not sufficient for robust low-FPR screening.
The complementary GPU$_1$ view is kept as a supporting diagnostic in the appendix (Figure~\ref{fig:appendix_gpu_tpr}), where near-1.0 values highlight collapse-prone regimes such as \texttt{bp-sc-norm}.
Since CAC$_1$ is class-1 specific, we additionally report cross-class alignment consistency in the appendix (Figure~\ref{fig:appendix_cac0_cac1}); the CAC$_0$--CAC$_1$ association is moderate (Pearson \(r=0.629\)), supporting class-specific reporting in the main text.
In the present run set, no model shows CAC$_1$ \(>\) CAC$_0$; if such class-1-dominant cases appear in future experiments and remain clinically competitive, they would be meaningful candidates for targeted positive-class analysis.

\section{Stage 3: Cross-model consistency and consensus sample selection}
Cross-model agreement is quantified on the full test fold via model$\times$model Spearman $\rho$ of prediction scores, capturing global ranking similarity.
Screening agreement is assessed using intersection over union (IoU) of each model's top-5\% set, and within-set ordering agreement using Kendall's $\tau$ on the overlapping subset.
For candidate prioritization, Kendall's $\tau$ is treated as the key stability check inside the shared screening region, while Spearman and IoU provide complementary global and set-level context.

\paragraph{Agreement structure.}
Figure~\ref{fig:overview_agreement_heatmaps} shows clear preprocessing-driven structure: agreement clusters primarily by preprocessing regime. Across all model pairs, median Spearman agreement is \(0.252\), but rises to \(0.381\) for same-preprocessing pairs versus \(0.203\) across different preprocessing regimes.
This effect is even stronger for matched track+preprocessing comparisons (median Spearman \(0.658\)).
Within-regime clustering is strongest for \texttt{bp-sc} (within-\texttt{bp-sc} median Spearman \(\approx 0.725\)), whereas \texttt{bp-sc-norm} shows very weak internal coherence (within-\texttt{bp-sc-norm} median Spearman \(\approx 0.092\), mean \(\approx 0.053\)).
Cross-regime pairs involving \texttt{bp-sc-norm} are similarly weak (median \(\approx 0.049\) vs.\ \texttt{bp}, \(\approx 0.124\) vs.\ \texttt{bp-sc}), with multiple negative correlations.
As expected, screening-set stability is stricter than global score correlation: median top-5\% IoU is \(0.175\) overall (\(0.280\) same-preprocessing vs.\ \(0.137\) different-preprocessing), and median within-overlap Kendall's \(\tau\) is \(0.143\) overall (\(0.284\) vs.\ \(0.086\)).
Thus, Spearman confirms global clustering by preprocessing, IoU quantifies reproducibility of the selected screening cohort, and Kendall's \(\tau\) quantifies stability of candidate ordering within that shared cohort.
The \(\tau\) contrast shows that ordering consistency inside shared candidates drops sharply across preprocessing regimes, which directly motivates using consensus thresholds before selecting representative XAI cases.
The strongest pairwise agreement occurs between \texttt{t1-exp03-bp-focal} and \texttt{t1-exp04-bp-focal-rot10} (\(\rho=0.910\)), whereas the weakest pair (\(\rho=-0.492\)) appears between two \texttt{bp-sc-norm} models from different tracks/objectives, indicating substantial reordering in that regime.
Notably, \texttt{t1-bp} aligns much more with \texttt{t3-bp-sc} than with \texttt{t3-bp}: median Spearman is \(0.709\) vs.\ \(0.311\), and median IoU is \(0.376\) vs.\ \(0.240\).
For IoU specifically, \texttt{t1-bp} overlaps more with \texttt{t1-bp-sc} (median \(0.403\)) than with \texttt{t1-bp} internal pairs (median \(0.295\)), indicating that preprocessing can preserve screening-set identity across some cross-track/cross-objective pairings.
Overall, IoU values are mostly below \(0.5\) (global median \(0.175\); typical ranges \(\approx 0.10\) to \(0.45\), maximum \(0.583\)), so exact top-5\% membership is only partially stable.
Practically, IoU here measures how many selected candidates are shared relative to the union of both model selections; low-to-moderate IoU therefore implies that many borderline samples around the 5\% cut-off are model-dependent.
A compact combined IoU/Kendall overview is shown in Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:appendix_iou_kendall}.

\paragraph{RRA-based robust sample prioritization.}
\textbf{Interpretation caution:} in this section, ``robust positive'' and ``robust negative'' refer to two RRA outputs (\texttt{RRA-pos} on original scores and \texttt{RRA-neg} on score-inverted rankings), \emph{not} to ground-truth Chagas labels.
To move from broad consensus pools to robust exemplar selection, we apply Robust Rank Aggregation (RRA) on full-test rankings across all models (\(N=73{,}236\)).
This yields \(7{,}411\) robust positives and \(11{,}456\) robust negatives, where ``robust'' means repeatedly extreme ranking across model lists rather than a single-model outlier.
RRA therefore complements top-5\% consensus: consensus captures repeated inclusion near a fixed screening threshold, whereas RRA emphasizes globally stable extremes across full rankings.
Figure~\ref{fig:rra_dataset_violin} and Table~\ref{tab:rra_kw} show clear dataset structure in these robust sets: robust positives are dominated by CODE-15 (92.97\%), with smaller PTB-XL (4.76\%) and SaMi-Trop (2.27\%) contributions, while robust negatives are mostly CODE-15 (84.03\%) and PTB-XL (15.87\%), with very little SaMi-Trop (0.10\%).
The Kruskal--Wallis tests are significant for both robust positives and robust negatives, confirming that RRA-rank distributions differ across datasets rather than being exchangeable.
Qualitatively, ridge shapes are also distinct: robust-positive densities concentrate toward the low-rank end (right side), robust-negative densities toward the high-rank end (left side), and PTB-XL/SaMi-Trop ridges appear narrower or multi-peaked relative to CODE-15, which is plausibly influenced by their much smaller subset sizes.
This concentration at the two extremes is expected by construction: \texttt{PyFLAGR} returns ranks for robustly aggregated items only, while the majority of test samples receive no RRA rank and are therefore absent from these ridge distributions.

\section{Stage 4: ST-DFT-LRP plausibility assessment}
For each run, per-sample ST-DFT-LRP outputs are stored in \texttt{stdftlrp\_beat\_agg.csv} and include:
(i) beat-aggregated 4$\times$4 time$\times$frequency relevance grids (mean and mass-weighted),
(ii) per-lead relevance mass allocations and lead-importance statistics, and
(iii) quality metadata for stratified aggregation and visualization.
In this thesis, beat-structure aggregation is performed on lead V2 (index~7), while lead-mass diagnostics are still computed over all 12 leads from the same per-sample relevance outputs.
All Stage-4 runs use the same ST-DFT-LRP setup as specified in Methods: \(f_s=400\)\,Hz, rectangular window \(H=125\), non-overlapping hop (\(D=H\)), and analysis restricted to 0.67--45\,Hz.

\paragraph{Cross-preprocessing robust-sample availability for XAI.}
RRA is computed on the full test fold (\(N=73{,}236\)), but ST-DFT-LRP is run on the probe subset.
To compare identical ECGs across preprocessing regimes, we require that a sample is present in all 24 lead-V2 ST-DFT-LRP outputs.
This all-run constraint retains 4{,}819 probe samples (from 4{,}893).
The resulting matched robust candidate lists are exported to \texttt{analysis/embeddings\_probe/xai\_summary/lead\_7/matched\_robust\_*\_all\_runs.csv}.

\paragraph{Summary of key results.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Preprocessing dominates performance:} \texttt{bp} and \texttt{bp-sc} yield competitive screening performance, while \texttt{bp-sc-norm} substantially degrades ranking utility across learning strategies.
  \item \textbf{Best clinical utility:} \texttt{t1-bp-focal} achieves the best TPR@5\% and also the best pAUC@5\% and AP among the evaluated runs; representation learning (\texttt{t3-bp-sc-supstd}) is competitive under \texttt{bp-sc}.
  \item \textbf{Rotation augmentation is detrimental:} \texttt{+rot10} consistently reduces performance, suggesting the employed frontal-plane rotation is not reliably label-preserving under the dataset and transform assumptions.
  \item \textbf{Label/quality confounding is measurable:} within CODE-15\%, Zhao2018 quality is significantly associated with the Chagas label, motivating explicit stratification and cautious interpretation of embedding overlays.
  \item \textbf{Embedding diagnostics support validity filtering:} healthier embedding geometry (GPU/CAC) generally co-occurs with better screening utility, supporting a hierarchical evaluation workflow before interpretability analyses.
  \item \textbf{Precordial lead emphasis is consistent:} lead-mass attribution concentrates on V1--V6 under mild preprocessing, motivating V2-based beat-structure attribution as a focused plausibility probe.
  \item \textbf{Matched robust-XAI coverage is limited but usable:} under the strict all-run lead-V2 constraint, 596 robust positives and 975 robust negatives remain available for same-sample cross-preprocessing comparisons.
  \item \textbf{4$\times$4 disease contrast weakens under stronger preprocessing:} median CODE15 disease deltas (\(\Delta\)QRS, \(\Delta\)Between, \(\Delta\)physio-ratio) are strongest in \texttt{bp}, reduced in \texttt{bp-sc}, and close to neutral in \texttt{bp-sc-norm}.
\end{itemize}
